# 02 — Be An Earth System Trend Detective!

## Confirmed challenge intent

NASA asks teams to choose variables measured by NASA missions or generated by NASA models, visualize how those variables change through time, and determine **what is changing, where, by how much, and whether the trend is statistically significant**. The summary explicitly warns that the same environmental process can produce different trend directions in different regions.

Sources:
- https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/
- https://nasaspaceapps.ae/challenges

## Why this is unusually well aligned with NeuraX

This challenge rewards exactly the combination NeuraX is strongest at: data analysis, statistics/ML, scientific reasoning, software, spatial visualization, and research-style interpretation. It also has a natural path from hackathon prototype to a more rigorous post-event project.

The important word is **significant**. A weak submission will calculate a linear slope and color a map. A strong submission will treat trend detection as a scientific inference problem and communicate uncertainty honestly.

## NeuraX product concept

### “TrendLens — an Earth-change investigation workbench”

A user chooses:

- variable;
- region of interest;
- time window;
- aggregation scale / season;

The system returns:

1. a spatial trend map;
2. a regional time-series;
3. magnitude and direction of trend;
4. statistical significance / uncertainty;
5. locations where the trend changes sign or accelerates;
6. a plain-language explanation grounded in the computed evidence.

A compelling demo could compare two regions where the same variable behaves differently, which directly mirrors the challenge statement.

## Candidate data families — verify against the official 2026 resource list

Potential NASA sources include, depending on the selected variable:

- MODIS / VIIRS products;
- GPM precipitation;
- SMAP soil moisture;
- GRACE / GRACE-FO water-mass anomalies;
- MERRA-2 reanalysis/model outputs;
- land-surface temperature, vegetation, snow/ice, aerosol, ocean or atmospheric products available through NASA Earthdata.

These are **candidate families, not yet confirmed challenge-required resources**. The full brief may narrow or suggest particular datasets.

## Scientific / analytical approach

### Baseline

For each grid cell or regional aggregate:

- clean missing/invalid values;
- normalize temporal resolution;
- optionally calculate seasonal/monthly anomalies;
- fit a simple trend model;
- calculate uncertainty/significance;
- visualize slope + confidence/significance mask.

### Methods worth considering

- linear regression with confidence interval;
- Mann–Kendall trend test for monotonic trends;
- Sen's slope as a robust effect-size estimate;
- seasonal Mann–Kendall where seasonality is strong;
- change-point detection as a stretch goal;
- multiple-testing correction if significance is computed over many pixels.

The exact method must match the statistical assumptions and the data. “AI” is not automatically better here; interpretable statistics may be scientifically stronger.

## AI/ML opportunity

AI can add value around the analysis rather than replace it:

- anomaly/change-point discovery;
- clustering regions by temporal behavior;
- automatically surfacing “interesting contrasts”;
- natural-language explanation generated **from structured computed results**, not from unconstrained model speculation;
- query interface such as “where has X increased fastest since year Y?” translated into reproducible analysis.

## 48-hour MVP

Do **not** support ten variables. Choose one scientifically interesting variable and make the analysis rigorous.

Suggested flow:

1. Pre-download or efficiently query one NASA dataset.
2. Select one global/regional variable with sufficient temporal history.
3. Compute seasonal or annual anomalies.
4. Calculate trend magnitude + significance.
5. Render map and click-through time-series.
6. Add two pre-selected contrast stories for the demo.
7. Document assumptions and limitations.

## Evaluation

Minimum scientific checks:

- synthetic time-series with known slope/no slope;
- sensitivity to time-window choice;
- missing-data handling;
- comparison of trend method(s) on a small sample;
- report effect size separately from p-value/significance;
- verify several regions manually from raw time-series.

## Risks

- Statistical mistakes, especially treating autocorrelated/seasonal environmental time-series as independent observations.
- Huge gridded datasets causing slow queries.
- Building a generic dashboard instead of answering a crisp scientific question.
- LLM-generated causal explanations that exceed what the data supports.
- “Significant” interpreted only as a p-value rather than scientific/practical significance.
